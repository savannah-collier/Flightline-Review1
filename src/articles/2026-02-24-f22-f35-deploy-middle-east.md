As tensions between the United States and Iran continue to rise, the U.S. has deployed more than 150 aircraft to bases across Europe and the Middle East. The buildup of air power in the region is now the largest since the 2003 invasion that marked the start of the Iraq War.

According to officials, the deployment includes multiple F-35 and F-22 Raptor fighter squadrons. In addition, reports indicate a second aircraft carrier—equipped with attack and electronic warfare aircraft—and a command-and-control platform are en route to the region. Satellite imagery appears to confirm large numbers of fighter jets positioned at bases in Jordan, Saudi Arabia, and Israel, along with forces staged for rapid activation.

Command-and-control aircraft serve as airborne operations centers, enabling real time coordination during military missions. Equipped with advanced communications and surveillance systems, these aircraft relay intelligence, support reconnaissance efforts, and help synchronize air and ground operations. They play a critical role in managing large-scale, sustained air campaigns.

At this time, President Donald Trump has not announced whether he will order a strike on Iran. Unlike the limited strike conducted in June against three Iranian nuclear sites, officials suggest the current buildup would allow the United States to sustain operations for weeks or even months, if directed.

White House briefings and the United States Department of Defense typically acknowledge major force movements but do not confirm impending military action for national security reasons. That pattern continues, as defense officials have confirmed the increase in regional air assets while declining to discuss specific operational plans.

The buildup follows the conclusion of U.S.–Iran nuclear negotiations in Geneva, which reportedly ended without significant progress. U.S. leaders have reiterated that Iran must not develop a nuclear weapon. Analysts view the deployment as a strategic positioning of forces—intended to maintain military options should diplomatic efforts fail.
